# Tests for ticket #10: show the taken photo on a temporary screen

Written from the ticket's "Done when" only, before the code exists.

**Where these run:** everything runs on the `Pixel_10` emulator. The ticket names no device, and the emulator can prove every item. Its camera stores photos the same way the Pixel 7's does (sideways, with a note saying which way is up), so "the right way up" is a real check. Its picture is still, but `adb emu rotate` turns its camera to another view of the room, which gives two different photos with nobody holding anything. `adb emu event send` puts two real fingers on its touch screen, which a real phone can't do from the Mac. The Pixel 7 isn't needed.

## How the integration tests prove it

The tests look at the app the way a person does, so they accept any way of building the photo screen: any name, any way of drawing the photo, any background, on the full screen or between the phone's bars.

- **"The photo screen"** means the app is in front, shows no words, has no buttons, and stays that way for three seconds. The list of what's on screen is empty for a moment whenever a screen closes, so a moment doesn't count.
- **"Shows the photo just taken"** compares the emulator's real screen with the real file. The test finds the newest photo inside the app, written after the shutter tap, then reads it on the Mac and compares it with a screenshot, square by square. A blank screen, or any other picture, fails.
- **"The home screen doesn't show"** is watched many times a second, from the shutter tap until the photo screen has lasted three seconds, so a home screen that only flashes by fails.
- **"The new photo, not the old one"** takes a photo, goes home, turns the emulator's camera a quarter turn so it sees another view, then takes a second photo in the same run. The screen must match the second photo and must not match the first. The app keeps its photo under the same name each time, so a screen that remembers the old picture by its name fails here. The camera is turned back at the end of the test, even if it fails.
- **"The whole photo fits"** means the photo is drawn at the largest size that fits the screen, or the space between the phone's bars, with nothing cut off. A cropped, stretched, zoomed or small photo doesn't match. Where the photo sits in the space left over is free.
- **"The right way up"** turns the file's picture the way the file's own note says, and compares that with the screen. A photo drawn on its side doesn't match. Manual step 1 shows it with a person's eyes.
- **"No text and no buttons"** reads the app's own list of what a person can see and tap. A description of the photo for screen readers isn't drawn, so it doesn't count as text. Something drawn that isn't in that list, such as a bare icon, is left to manual step 1.
- **"Pinches"** puts two real fingers on the emulator's touch screen and slides them apart, then in another test together. **"Double-taps"** is two real quick taps in the middle of the screen. Screenshots are compared before the gesture and after it, and for a pinch also while the fingers are still down, so a photo that zooms and then springs back fails too.
- **A gesture must really land.** "Nothing changed" would also be true if the fingers never arrived, so the app counts every finger that reaches it, and a test fails if the fingers didn't.
- **"The home screen shows"** after back means the app is in front and shows the three sketch 1 texts, after exactly one back. It must still be there three seconds later. A camera screen left underneath the photo fails, because back would land on it.
- **"Then the camera screen opens"** taps the real button after leaving each way, and Android must say the app is using the camera.
- **"No photo screen appears"** watches from the back until three seconds after the home screen shows. An earlier photo is taken first, so a photo screen that opens whenever the camera closes fails: the screen must never show that photo with no words and no buttons around it.

## Changes to ticket #8's tests

This ticket replaces one thing #8 asked for: after the shutter, the app now goes to the photo screen, not home. So `app/integration_test/camera_driver.dart` no longer expects the home screen after the shutter, and its test `Given the camera screen is open, when the person taps the shutter, then the photo is kept inside the app and the home screen shows` is now called `... then the photo is kept inside the app`. Everything else about those tests stays as it was. The shutter, shutter-tap and kept-photo helpers moved from that file to `helpers/driven_app.dart`, so both tickets use one copy.

## Running the automated tests

Start the emulator with `fvm flutter emulators --launch Pixel_10`. From `app/`:

```sh
export ANDROID_SERIAL=emulator-5554
fvm flutter drive -d "$ANDROID_SERIAL" \
  --driver=integration_test/photo_screen_driver.dart \
  --target=integration_test/camera_app.dart
```

Don't touch the emulator during a run. If a run is stopped by force in the middle of the "new photo" test, the emulator's camera stays turned. To put it back, run `adb -s emulator-5554 emu rotate` until the camera screen shows the room upright, with the TV standing on its cabinet.

These tests are not picked up by `fvm flutter test integration_test`, because only the Mac can read the kept photo and turn the emulator's camera.

## Manual steps

Screenshots go in `docs/evidence/10/`. To take one, from the repo root: `adb -s emulator-5554 exec-out screencap -p > docs/evidence/10/<name>.png`.

### Before you start

1. Start the emulator: `fvm flutter emulators --launch Pixel_10`.
2. Install the app: from `app/`, run `fvm flutter run -d emulator-5554`, then stop it with `q`.

### Step 1: how the photo screen looks

1. Open **Dò Vé Số** from the app list.
2. Tap **Quét vé**. If Android asks, tap **While using the app**.
3. Wait three seconds, then take the screenshot `camera-before-shutter.png`.
4. Tap the shutter.
5. Wait three seconds, then take the screenshot `photo-screen.png`.
6. Compare the two screenshots.

**Should happen:** `photo-screen.png` shows the room from the camera screen, and only that.

- The room is upright: the TV stands on its cabinet, and the floor is at the bottom.
- The whole photo fits on the screen. It shows at least everything inside the dashed frame in `camera-before-shutter.png`, and it's as wide as the screen.
- No text, no buttons, no icons, no frame. The phone's own status bar and bottom line can stay. The red "DEBUG" ribbon in the corner comes from the test build, not from the screen.

There's no sketch for this screen, so nothing to compare it with.

**Screenshots:** `camera-before-shutter.png`, `photo-screen.png`.
