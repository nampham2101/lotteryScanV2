**No widget tests.** A widget test draws the app on the Mac with no phone to turn, so it cannot prove anything about turning the phone.

Files:

- Integration tests (run on the Mac, they turn the emulator): `app/integration_test/portrait_lock_driver.dart`
- The real app with a way for those tests to ask what its screen shows: `app/integration_test/portrait_lock_app.dart`
- Turning and reading the emulator with adb: `app/integration_test/helpers/phone.dart`

## How the integration tests prove it

- **Turning the phone** sets the emulator's accelerometer, which is what a real turn changes. Android then decides by itself whether to turn the screen, as on a real phone.
- **The phone really was turned.** Each test reads the accelerometer back, so a pass never comes from a turn that didn't happen.
- **Auto-rotate is switched on** for the run, and put back as it was afterwards. With it off, no app turns, so the tests could pass without the lock.
- **"Upright"** means Android reports the screen at 0 degrees with the app in front. Criteria 1 and 3 also ask the app itself: it must be drawn on a tall screen, not a wide one.
- **"Doesn't turn"** means Android logged no screen turn at any moment during the test, not only at the end. For criterion 2 that includes while the app opens, so the app must never show sideways, not even briefly.
- **"Nothing has changed"** (criterion 3) means the app shows the same texts, in the same places, at the same size, as before the phone was turned, in the same run of the app (not restarted).
- **Opening the app** (criterion 2): the app is fully closed first, then opened with the same request the launcher sends when you tap the icon. Step 2 below taps the real icon.

## Running the automated tests

From `app/`, with the `Pixel_10` emulator running (`fvm flutter emulators --launch Pixel_10`):

```sh
fvm flutter drive -d emulator-5554 \
  --driver=integration_test/portrait_lock_driver.dart \
  --target=integration_test/portrait_lock_app.dart
```

These tests are not picked up by `fvm flutter test integration_test`, because only the Mac can turn the emulator.

## Manual steps

Screenshots go in `docs/evidence/5/`. Two kinds:

- **Phone screen:** what the phone's screen shows. From the repo root: `adb exec-out screencap -p > docs/evidence/5/<name>.png`. An upright screen gives a tall picture with the text reading normally; a screen that turned gives a wide picture.
- **Emulator window:** a Mac screenshot of the whole emulator window, so the turned phone is visible too: press Cmd+Shift+4, then Space, then click the emulator window. Save it as `docs/evidence/5/<name>.png`.

### Before you start

1. Start the `Pixel_10` emulator and install the app: from `app/`, run `fvm flutter run -d emulator-5554`, then stop it with `q`.
2. Make sure auto-rotate is on: swipe down twice from the top of the emulator screen and check the **Auto-rotate** tile is on. (Or run `adb shell settings get system accelerometer_rotation`, which should print `1`.)
3. Make sure the emulator is upright: the phone in the emulator window stands tall.

The emulator's side toolbar has **Rotate left** and **Rotate right** buttons. Each turns the phone a quarter turn.

### Step 1: the screen stays upright when the phone is turned sideways

1. Open **Dò Vé Số** from the app list, with the phone upright.
2. Click **Rotate left** once. Wait three seconds.
3. Take both screenshots: `turned-left-window.png` (emulator window) and `turned-left-screen.png` (phone screen).
4. Click **Rotate right** once, to stand the phone up again.
5. Click **Rotate right** once more. Wait three seconds.
6. Take both screenshots: `turned-right-window.png` and `turned-right-screen.png`.
7. Click **Rotate left** once, to stand the phone up again.

**Should happen:** when the phone turns, the app's screen turns with the phone, as if glued to it. Inside the emulator window the app's text now runs along the phone's long side, sideways to you. The screen does not spin round to face you, and there is no turning animation or flash. The phone-screen screenshots are tall pictures with the text reading normally.

**Screenshots:** `turned-left-window.png`, `turned-left-screen.png`, `turned-right-window.png`, `turned-right-screen.png`.

### Step 2: the app opens upright when the phone is already sideways

1. Close the app: open the recent apps (the square button, or swipe up and hold), and swipe **Dò Vé Số** away.
2. With the phone upright on the phone's home screen, click **Rotate left** once.
3. Open the app list and tap **Dò Vé Số**. Watch the screen closely while it opens.
4. Wait three seconds, then take both screenshots: `opened-left-window.png` and `opened-left-screen.png`.
5. Close the app as in 1, click **Rotate right** twice (the phone is now sideways the other way), and repeat 3 and 4 with the names `opened-right-window.png` and `opened-right-screen.png`.
6. Click **Rotate left** once, to stand the phone up again.

**Should happen:** each time, the app opens upright relative to the phone, the same way it opens when the phone is upright. It never shows sideways, not even for a moment while it opens, and it does not turn after opening. The phone-screen screenshots are tall pictures with the text reading normally.

**Screenshots:** `opened-left-window.png`, `opened-left-screen.png`, `opened-right-window.png`, `opened-right-screen.png`.

### Step 3: turning back upright is normal

1. Open **Dò Vé Số** with the phone upright. Take a phone-screen screenshot: `before-turn.png`.
2. Click **Rotate left** once. Wait three seconds.
3. Click **Rotate right** once, to stand the phone up again. Wait three seconds.
4. Take a phone-screen screenshot: `turned-back.png`.

**Should happen:** after turning back, the app looks exactly as it did before the turn: upright, same texts in the same places, same size. It did not restart (no blank screen or opening screen) and shows no message, popup or error. `before-turn.png` and `turned-back.png` look the same, apart from the clock in the status bar.

**Screenshots:** `before-turn.png`, `turned-back.png`.
